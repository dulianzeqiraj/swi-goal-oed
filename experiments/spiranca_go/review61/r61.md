## 1. Outcome

- **Twenty papers, five per institution**, give stakeholders mathematical tools for normal operation, intense rainfall, drought and causal cases (Section 2). Each one changes a definite part of our model: an input, a boundary, the goal, the margin or the decision rule; each card ends with an innovation.
- **Extremes change the model in three ways** (Section 3): they go beyond the record (drought), they break the link between what is recorded and what acts (intense rain that runs off), and they change a causal mechanism (farmers who pump far more in a drought they never saw).
- **Innovation, ROJA-GO (the watch).** Extremes speed up the drift that re-anchoring must follow. The optimal re-anchoring window then shrinks to the last sample (Proposition R1), and an extra chloride sample is taken when a detector that needs no chloride fires.
- **Test on the variable-density section (20 aquifers, 6 weather sequences each).** Without action the screen exceeds the standard in 25% of the seasons in ordinary weather and in 52%, 59% and 50% under the drought, the causal change and the compound event. With the decision rule "cut pumping after an alarm", the ROJA-GO watch keeps exceedance to 4.7%, 9.1% and 4.3% (yearly sample alone: 5.9%, 11.5%, 5.1%; static alarm: 15.0%, 21.2%, 13.1%), with 73% of the planned water delivered in the drought.
- **The alarm stays valid in the extremes.** In open loop the ROJA-GO alarm under-warns in 2.7%–4.3% of the stress seasons (target ≤ 10%), against 3.5%–8.3% with the yearly sample alone; under the causal change the aquifers above the target fall from 6 to 2 of 20, for 6 extra samples in 30 years.

## 4. Innovation: ROJA-GO, the watch that samples when an extreme leaves the record

After the decision, the alarm error is $\hat Y_t-Y_t=-\eta_t+d_t$, where $d_t$ is the drift the record could not foresee, with $|d_t-d_s|\le\rho|t-s|$. Re-anchoring with the mean error of the last $K$ samples, taken $\Delta,2\Delta,\dots,K\Delta$ before $t$, leaves a lag of at most $\rho\Delta(K+1)/2$ (Proposition S5 of the SPIRANCA-GO note). An extreme makes $\rho$ large.

**Proposition R1 (the window shrinks with the drift).** With independent noise of variance $\sigma^2$,

$$\mathbb{E}\left[(\hat Y_t-\bar e_{t,K}-Y_t)^2\right]\le\sigma^2\left(1+\frac{1}{K}\right)+\frac{\rho^2\Delta^2(K+1)^2}{4},$$

and the bound is smallest at the $K$ that solves $K^2(K+1)=2\sigma^2/(\rho\Delta)^2$. In particular, if the drift between two samples is at least the noise, $\rho\Delta\ge\sigma$, then $K_{\mathrm{opt}}=1$: use the last sample only. *Proof.* Noise and lag terms are bounded separately (S5); the derivative of the bound in $K$ is $-\sigma^2/K^2+\rho^2\Delta^2(K+1)/2$, which is increasing, and at $K=1$ it is $\rho^2\Delta^2-\sigma^2\ge 0$. ∎

**The watch.** Every season, without any chloride: (i) the gauge leaves the range of the record (one season or the two-year filter), or (ii) the head residuals, after removing what the anchors explain, jump by more than anything in the record (the trigger of paper 20). When either fires, one extra chloride sample is taken (at most one extra a year, the budget of paper 11), and the window is $K=1$ while the extreme lasts or while two consecutive samples differ by more than three record standard deviations (R1). Otherwise $K=3$ and one sample a year, as in S5.

**The storylines.** All five use the same weather, so only the extreme differs. The drought averages −2 sd for six years; the worst six years of the 80-year record averaged -1.1 sd. The intense rainfall puts +2.5 sd on the gauge for two years, of which only a fifth infiltrates. In the causal change the farmers respond to the drought 3 times as strongly as in the record.

**The stakeholder decision (closed loop).** The utility pumps the decided +0.3 unless the alarm sounded in the previous season; then it pumps -0.3 (the risk-threshold rule of paper 7). For comparison: no action; a static alarm; yearly re-anchoring; and a continuous chloride sensor at the screen that cuts pumping when chloride reaches 89% of the standard.

**What the test shows.** (1) The extremes, not the decision, now drive the risk: the drought multiplies the exceeding seasons by 2.1 and the causal change by 2.3. (2) The detectors fire in 49% of the seasons of an extreme and 9% outside it, so the extra samples go where the drift is. (3) In the three drought storylines a continuous sensor that reacts at 89% of the standard leaves 20% of the seasons above it, against 6% with ROJA-GO: the aquifer has memory, so acting at the standard is late, and the certified margin acts earlier. (4) The certificate is still needed: least squares with the same watch under-warns in 25% of the stress seasons.

**For Fushë-Kuqe.** Before a drought season, agree the rule with the stakeholders in this form: pump as decided while the alarm is silent; cut when it sounds; take one chloride sample a year at the supply screen, and one more as soon as the rain gauge or the piezometers leave the range of the record. Report with every decision the margin, the samples spent and the confounding strength $\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}$ that would overturn it (paper 5).

## 5. Verification and caveats

- The network of this session blocked Crossref, doi.org, arXiv and several publisher hosts. Every DOI and affiliation was therefore confirmed from publisher or repository records found by search (ORA, CaltechAUTHORS, Harvard DASH, DSpace@MIT, PMC, RePEc, ADS), not from Crossref. Pages of the NeurIPS, UAI and PASC papers and some article numbers are not verified.
- Numbers flagged as uncertain in the notes are not used here: Wu et al. (2024) report two different intervals in two versions; per-region numbers of Kew et al. (2021) and the coincidence probabilities of Murgatroyd & Hall (2020) were not retrieved.
- Kurth et al. (2023) and Frederikse et al. (2020) have Caltech authors together with NVIDIA and JPL authors. None of the twenty papers is about seawater intrusion; their use here is our transfer, and the test is on a synthetic 2-D section, not on field data.
- Code: experiments/spiranca_go/sx_run.py (storylines, watch, closed loop); results res/sx.json. All 20 papers are new to this series: none is in the 3,162 references of the earlier reviews.
