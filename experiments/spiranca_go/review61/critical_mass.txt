Range of expertise. The challenge needs eleven competences, and the network was assembled against that list. Physics and method: variable-density flow and transport modelling; hydrogeochemistry of intrusion; Bayesian inverse methods, experimental design and statistical learning from records; multi-criteria decision analysis. Aquifers: coastal karst; alluvial and deltaic systems; geophysical imaging of the fresh–saline interface. Practice and custody: managed aquifer recharge; long-term monitoring records (geological surveys); well-field operation and pumping records (water utilities); status classification (water agencies and river basin authorities).

Critical mass. The network comprises 58 proposers from more than twenty COST countries, about 60% from Inclusiveness Target Countries; more than half are young researchers and innovators and close to 40% are women. About 90% are universities and research organisations; the rest are governmental bodies and a water utility that operates a coastal well field and brings pumping records and operators' logs. All eleven competences are present, the methodological ones in several independent groups.

Plan to grow. Three gaps are stated. Applied mathematics and statistics are not yet any proposer's core field: at least three such groups will be recruited by Month 12. At least two more utilities will join Working Group 4 by Month 12, with consultancies and instrument makers in a Stakeholder Advisory Panel. Operating recharge schemes are scarce, so their role is kept light. New sites are recruited for contrast in hydrogeological setting, not convenience, and every member has a defined role in a Working Group deliverable.
