1. Extremes beyond the record. Record-breaking droughts and intense rainfall push the aquifer into states the record never contained; in our tests, a record-only alarm failed after the pumping decision in 9 of 20 aquifers.
2. Hidden human feedback. Unregistered private wells pump more in a drought; this changes the causal mechanism, is invisible in the records and must be bounded rather than estimated.
3. Coupled, slow physics at low cost. Density-driven flow, salt memory in clay layers and ion exchange act over decades, yet every decision must be checked over many storylines; this needs a coupled Newton solver, an exact adjoint and goal-oriented simplification.
4. Sparse data. Chloride is sampled rarely at few wells, piezometers are few, and sea level and recharge are uncertain; where and when to sample is part of the problem.
5. From mathematics to decisions. Stakeholders must agree on the acceptable share of seasons above 250 mg/L, on who gives up water, utility or farmers, and on the rules that trigger pumping cuts; the safest tested rule delivers 71% of the planned water in the worst storyline.
6. Validation and transfer. Results from synthetic 2-D aquifers must be confirmed with Fushë-Kuqe field data, extended to 3-D and transferred to other coastal and karst aquifers.
7. Open-source software. No open tool lets an authority or utility run these checks itself; members contribute code, and missions and Training Schools assemble it into one documented open-source workflow whose minimal version needs no adjoint solver or geochemical model.
